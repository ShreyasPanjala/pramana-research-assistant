A neural network is a machine learning model inspired by the human brain. It uses connected nodes, or artificial neurons, arranged in layers to process data, recognize patterns, and make predictions without explicit step-by-step instructions. You can explore the IBM Neural Network Guide for a deeper technical overview.Key ComponentsInput layer: Receives raw data like pixels, words, or numbers.Hidden layers: Middle layers that perform mathematical operations, multiplying inputs by weights and adding biases.Output layer: Generates the final prediction, classification, or decision.Weights and biases: Internal parameters adjusted during training to reduce errors.Activation function: A math rule applied by each neuron to introduce non-linearity, helping the model learn complex relationships.This short video breaks down the basic architecture and flow of information inside a neural network:1:10How Neural Networks Work (Simple Overview)15 days agoYouTube · PhoenixNAP Global IT ServicesHow They LearnForward propagation: Data moves forward from the input, through the hidden layers, to the output.Loss calculation: An error function measures how far the prediction is from the real answer.Backpropagation: The network works backward, updating weights and biases to fix mistakes and improve accuracy over time.Common Types and UsesFeedforward (FNN): Basic networks for simple classification and numbers.Convolutional (CNN): Specialized for images, video recognition, and computer vision.Recurrent (RNN): Built for text, speech, and sequential data.If you'd like, let me know:Are you trying to build or train a specific model?Do you need help with math concepts like backpropagation or activation functions?I can provide a more tailored explanation or code example.IBMWhat Is a Neural Network?
